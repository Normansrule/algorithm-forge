Tower of Hanoi

Move the whole stack from peg A to peg C, one disk at a time, never putting a bigger disk on a smaller one. The recursive solution needs exactly 2ⁿ − 1 moves, and you can watch every call that produces them.

What's happening

How to read this

  • Pegs: disk k is labeled k; bigger number = bigger disk. The red outline marks the disk that just moved, and the arrow shows where it came from.
  • Recursion tree: each node is one call Hanoi(k, from→to). Its left child moves the k − 1 smaller disks out of the way, the node itself moves disk k, and its right child moves the k − 1 disks back on top. Reading the nodes left to right gives the moves in order, which is why every node sits exactly above its own move.
  • Call stack: the chain of calls that are still unfinished. Its height is at most n, even though there are 2ⁿ − 1 calls in total: time is exponential, memory is linear.
  • Counters: the moves so far next to 2ⁿ − 1, the number that the recurrence M(n) = 2M(n−1) + 1, M(1) = 1 predicts.

Try this

  1. Watch n = 3 and write down the 7 moves. Then switch to "Play it yourself" and reproduce them from memory.
  2. With n = 4, pause right after disk 4 moves (move 8). Where are disks 1–3? (All on peg B: exactly what the first recursive call promised.)
  3. Notice that disk 1 moves on every odd-numbered move, always cycling in the same direction. Can you use this to solve n = 5 by hand?
  4. Play n = 4 yourself and try to finish in fewer than 15 moves. You can't: 2ⁿ − 1 is also a lower bound. The biggest disk must move at least once, and just before that the n − 1 smaller disks must all sit on the third peg, which already costs 2ⁿ⁻¹ − 1 moves, and the same again afterwards.
  5. Slide n from 1 to 8 and watch the tree double each time: 1, 3, 7, 15, …, 255 calls.
